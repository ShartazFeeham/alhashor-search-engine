# Judge 1 (skeptical senior engineer): subsequence index or something else?

Read-only on the project. All of my re-computations are in `decision/j1/` (`load.mjs`, `cap.mjs`, `infl.mjs`, `fam.mjs`, `drop.mjs`, `bucket.mjs`, `wl.mjs`, `eq.mjs`, `subn.mjs`, `fold.mjs`, `fp.mjs`, `phantom.mjs`, `t.mjs`, `raw3.mjs`, each with its `.out` or printed result). They read the real `public/json/tags` and `substring` and never write to the project.

## 0. One-paragraph verdict

Do not build a subsequence index, and do not build subsequence as a "did you mean" tier yet. It is the right tool for one failure (letters dropped from a word you typed) and that failure is already covered at equal recall by edit distance 1 over an in-memory word list, which also reaches swaps, insertions and wrong letters. A static key-to-words subsequence map cannot serve a typo at all (a typo is never a key). What the data actually says is: (1) the biggest measured problem is not search quality but the substring expansion fetching the tags file of every containing word, which a cap of 50 words fixes (average files 28 to 7, p99 675 to 39, 99% of hadis kept), and which becomes mandatory the moment you shard by 3 letters; (2) a single prefix-agnostic word list (232 to 351 KB gzipped, built by a 20-line script, used only in memory) fixes the first-letters problem, which 3-letter shards make clearly worse (48 to 68% of typos land outside the typed word's file, against 33 to 49% at 2 letters), gives did-you-mean without downloads, gives autocomplete, and can replace the 1,164 substring files; (3) letter folding and ending stripping are worth doing as in-memory groups built from that list, with strict guards. Order: relevance test and measurement harness, cap the expansion, word list plus did-you-mean from it, then 3-letter shards, then folding and ending stripping behind a flag. Subsequence stays a 30-line function you can add later on the same list if the test set shows a measured gap.

## 2. Where the advisors and the lead disagree: who is right

1. **Is subsequence worth building?** Lead: no. Adv 2: no (D only). Adv 3: no, not even D. Adv 1: not as an index; a live appended tier adds +8 points at rank 20, all of it on two-letters-dropped and vowel-skeleton typing. Right: the lead and Adv 3, with Adv 1's number as the honest upper bound. Adv 1's own caveat (about 3 points if multi-letter omissions are 5% of failed queries) means the tier is worth a 30-line function once a test set says so, not an index and not a data file. Adv 3's "I measured no case where it adds a result the skeleton plus edit-1 does not" is too strong (drop2 is such a case) but the case is rare in real typing.
2. **Static file versus in-memory.** Adv 1 and Adv 2 (in memory from one list) beat Adv 3's separate 374 KB fold map and 75 KB skeleton map and the lead's "folded variant index" and "precomputed edit-1 neighbourhood". Everything those maps hold can be derived from the list in about 100 ms at load, with no generator and no file to keep in step. Adv 2 is right that B-full (+17,031 files, +6.6 MB gzipped) is indefensible.
3. **Delete index (Adv 1: build at load, 133 ms, 49 MB heap) versus linear scan (Adv 2: 1 to 15 ms).** Adv 2 is right: the scan needs no memory and no build time. Adv 1's delete index at load is over-engineering for 77k words.
4. **Replace the substring folder by a scan (Adv 1) or keep it at 2 letters (Adv 2) or shard it at 3 (the earlier decision).** Replacing by the scan is right in the end (claim 10: 97% identical; only misses the 3% raw-spelling quirks, and adds fragment matching) but it changes behaviour (a typed fragment such as "মুসলম" would start returning words containing it instead of nothing and a suggestion). Do it behind the same flag as everything else and A/B it on the test set. Sharding substring/ at 3 letters is the worst of the three options: 6,123 files with a 3 KB p99, for no measurable benefit, and then deleted if the scan lands.
5. **Cap the expansion.** Only Adv 2 raised it, and Adv 2 is right and verified (claim 3). The lead did not mention it. Adv 1's remark that "the 500-hadis cap stays" is inaccurate: that cap only applies to queries of 8 or more words. Adv 2's "credits per request, not bandwidth" claim is plausible and my request counts agree.
6. **Silent fold versus suggest-only.** Adv 3's split (silent for ি/ী ু/ূ ই/ঈ উ/ঊ ণ/ন য়/য ঙ ৎ hasanta ঁ ঃ at 4+ letters; suggest-only for শ/ষ/স and জ/য; never for aspirates, ড়, ত/ট) is the only guard-railed proposal. Adv 1's "B-wide" (100% on confusions at rank 20) is the over-folding trap: it aggressively merges words that are really distinct. Adv 3 is right, and the lead's "fold equivalent letters" must mean Adv 3's table, not the lead's own earlier list (the shipped `CONFUSED_PAIRS` has ছ/স, ত/ট, ো/ু).
7. **Where Adv 1 and Adv 3 differ on ending stripping.** Adv 3 wants stem 4+ letters (or 3+ letters with 50+ hadis) and the stem must be a vocabulary word. Adv 1 used up to two passes with a short list. Adv 3's rule is the safer one (about 1 in 24 false stems at 4 letters, 5 in 24 at 3, as judged by eye).

## 3. What everyone missed

1. **3-letter sharding makes typo recovery worse, and nobody measured by how much.** Typo changes the first two letters in 42.5% of drops, 33% of insertions, 49% of swaps; the first three letters in 59%, 48% and 68% (`bucket.mjs`, 2,000 non-word typos each, hadis-weighted). Today's `suggest` only compares against the typed word's own file, so going to 3 letters raises the share of typos it cannot reach from roughly 40% to roughly 55%. The word list is therefore not a nice-to-have next to the sharding; it is its prerequisite. Ship the list before the 3-letter shards, or in the same release.
2. **Sharding to 3 letters without the cap is a request-count regression for common words** (claim 4: "কে" 468 to 1,334 files). Adv 2 says it; the decision as written ("shard by 3 letters, keep 2-letter files behind switches") does not include the cap. The two belong together: shard and cap, or neither. With the cap, 3-letter shards are a clear win (average 74 KB against 219 KB, p99 files 39 against 30, p99 423 KB against 1,004 KB, at cap 50).
3. **Whole-vocabulary edit-1 raises noisy suggestions on correct words.** Of 400 sampled rare correct words (1 to 2 hadis, 4+ letters; this is 44% of the vocabulary), 49.3% have a whole-vocabulary edit-1 neighbour that meets today's rule (hadis at least 2 times the typed word's plus 1), against 39.5% when restricted to the typed word's 2-letter file and 34.8% for a 3-letter file (`fp.mjs`). Many of these are corpus misspellings, so some suggestions are useful, but the trigger (fewer than 3 hadis) fires exactly on these words. Nobody measured the false-suggestion rate; the relevance set must include correctly spelled rare words, and the new tier needs a stricter threshold (for example candidate hadis at least 5 times and at least 10).
4. **The 3-letter lookup is not just a different shard size.** `rawPrefixes` hard-codes a 4-character start and `substring(0, 2)`, and each raw spelling of য়, ড়, ঢ়, ো, ৌ inside the first three code points is a separate candidate file. I simulated it: lookups needing more than one file go from 6.6% (2 letters) to 10.2% (3 letters), average 1.066 to 1.103 files; 0.1% of lookups (0.6% of words) name a file that does not exist, and `loadFile` deletes failed files from its cache so those 404s are refetched on every search. Small, but it must be handled, ideally by normalising spelling in the generator (Adv 2's recommendation) rather than in the lookup.
5. **Keeping 2- and 3-letter modes behaviourally identical is a testing problem, not a flag.** Words of fewer than 3 code points have no 3-letter file, so the 3-letter mode needs the 2-letter files for them and for substring keys of 2 letters (the biggest expansions: "কে", "রা", "তে"). Results must be identical except for the cap. Write a golden test that runs the same 200 queries through both modes with a counting loader (the index already accepts an injected `load`) and asserts equal hadis sets and ranking; do not trust a manual comparison.
6. **Cap semantics.** Capped hadis are the non-exact ones, but ranking counts words matched, so in a multi-word query the lost 1% can be the hadis that match the second word only through a rare containing word. Hence the cap must be per word, applied after the typed word's own tags are included, and the "words matched" ranking tested on multi-word queries. "তে" loses 8% at cap 50 (the worst particle I checked); decide whether particles (2-letter words) get a higher cap such as 100.
7. **Over-folding risk is real but bounded and concentrated in a few tokens.** 3 genuine collisions confirmed (বাধা/বাঁধা, পূর্ণ/পূরণ, আসতে/আস্তে); 26 of the 176 big groups are visarga artefacts, which are a data cleaning problem (trailing ঃ glued by the source) better fixed once at build than folded forever. Highlighting (`matchPattern.js`) must learn the fold or users will see hits with nothing highlighted; Adv 3 notes it, nobody scoped it.
8. **Worker complexity.** Each addition (list, fold groups, ending map, skeleton groups, scan) lives in the worker next to the existing `searchIndex.js` (227 lines) and `didYouMean.js` (142). The sensible limit is: one list file, three derived in-memory maps (fold groups, ending map, skeleton groups; roughly 100 to 300 ms at load in Node, so 0.5 to 1.5 s on a phone, only built when a search is weak), pure functions in `src/lib/`, no new loader code paths. A subsequence index would add a second loader path and a third file family; that is the complexity cost the lead and Adv 2 point at, and I agree.
9. **The generator question.** No script builds `tags/`, so any new shard or list must read the existing `tags/*.json` as its source of truth. Rebuilding tags from the hadis texts reproduces only 93.8% of tokens (Adv 2); do not start that now. One script `scripts/build-search-index.mjs` (read tags, normalise, write 3-letter shards, sorted substring values, the word list) is the right size.
10. **Roman (Avro) typing already exists on the site** (redesign-plan section 12: curated list, then the Avro library). Adv 3's Avro findings (inserted hasanta, missing ো and া) therefore apply to a live path; the skeleton idea (derived in memory) is the likeliest sound fix for that, not subsequence.

## 4. Ranked recommendation (concrete plan)

Rule for every step: behind a flag or a separately reversible commit, no push, no deploy (per your standing rule), measured against the harness from step 0.

**Step 0. Measurement harness and test set (half a day).**
- Build `scripts/measure-search.mjs` that runs `createSearchIndex(load)` with a disk loader that counts requests and bytes (gzip each file once, cache). Fixed query sets: 200 hadis-weighted words (1 and 3 word queries), the 8 worst particles ("কে", "রা", "তে", "কর", ...), 30 to 50 real queries from TODO item 2 with expected top-10 hadis, and a synthetic typo set reusing `decision/a1/sim.js` (drop, swap, insert, confusable letters, inflection, first-letters, Avro misses) and a false-suggestion set of correct rare words.
- Record the "before" numbers: files and gzipped KB per query (average, p50, p99), recall at rank 1, 5, 20 per corruption type, suggestion rate on correct rare words.

**Step 1. Cap the substring expansion at the top 50 containing words by hadis count (smallest slice with the largest value).**
- One-off transformation of the existing `substring/*.json` (values sorted by count descending, same files, same keys; no new generator logic); 10-line change in `tagsForWord` to take the first 50 (always keep the typed word's own tags; consider cap 100 for 2-letter words).
- Expected, verified on simulation: average files 16 to 6 (2-letter) with p99 306 to 30; bytes 395 to 219 KB average; 99% of hadis kept; 0.9% of queries lose more than 10%.
- Success test: relevance set top-10 unchanged for the 30 to 50 queries; multi-word ranking unchanged on 100 two- and three-word queries.

**Step 2. Word list plus did-you-mean from memory.**
- `scripts/build-wordlist.mjs` (about 20 lines): normalised words with distinct hadis counts, sorted and front-coded: about 232 KB gzipped (351 KB plain). One request, fetched lazily by the worker only when `suggest` runs (and for autocomplete when TODO 6 is done); cached for 24 h. Do not ship the 75 KB skeleton map, the 374 KB fold map or any delete-neighbourhood file.
- Replace `vocabularyOf` (a tags-file download per weak word, up to 7 files) with the list: `closeWords` over the full list (use a length prefilter; 2 to 8 ms in Node), candidate `hits` from the count column, no downloads until a chip is clicked. Keep the order synonym, variant, close. Keep MAX_SUGGESTIONS at 3. For the new whole-vocabulary close tier use a stricter threshold (candidate hadis at least 5 times the typed word's and at least 10) because of the false-suggestion result in section 3.
- This directly fixes TODO item 5 and the first-letters weakness that step 3 would worsen.
- Measure: typo recovery per type with the new path against the old one (expect drop, swap and insert to rise toward Adv 1's 92 to 97% at rank 20), suggestion rate on correct rare words, bytes and files per weak search.

**Step 3. 3-letter shards for tags/ (and keep substring at 2 letters or retire it).**
- Only after steps 1 and 2. Generator: `scripts/build-search-index.mjs` reading `tags/*.json`, normalising spelling (so `rawPrefixes` can shrink to one candidate), writing 3-letter tags shards; words under 3 code points keep their 2-letter file. Substring: do not shard at 3 letters; either keep as is (step 1 sorted) or retire it in step 5.
- Parameterise `rawPrefixes` by shard length; golden test that 2-letter and 3-letter modes return identical hadis and ranking on the 200-query set (except for the cap).
- Measure: files and KB at p50 and p99 against step 1. Expectation at cap 50: average KB per search 219 to 74, p99 1,004 to 423, p99 files about 30 to 39.
- Keep the 2-letter files only while the golden test runs; delete them afterwards (the 18,587-file double set costs 14.5 MB gzipped of deploy and nothing at runtime).

**Step 4. Folding and ending stripping, in memory, behind a flag, Adv 3's rules.**
- Fold groups and ending map built from the list at load: silent tier ি/ী, ু/ূ, ই/ঈ, উ/ঊ, ণ/ন, য়/য, ঙ, ৎ, hasanta, ঁ, trailing ঃ, only for words of 4+ letters; strip trailing ঃ and glued dashes in the generator instead. Endings only with stem of 4+ letters (or 3+ letters and 50+ hadis), stem must be in the list, longest ending first, no verb endings, no খানা.
- Suggest-tier only: শ/ষ/স, জ/য (these exist in `CONFUSED_PAIRS` already). Never: aspirates, ড়/র, ত/ট, dropping া, ছ/স, ো/ু as silent.
- Rank the typed spelling first, label the variants in the UI (Adv 3's one muted line), extend `matchPattern.js` highlighting.
- Measure: inflection and letter-confusion rows in the synthetic set, plus a precision read of the 176 big groups (by eye, sample of 50 results) and the false-suggestion set. Ship only if the relevance set does not regress.

**Step 5 (optional, after step 3 settles). Retire `substring/` by an in-memory scan of the list.**
- Same flag. Measures: identical hadis sets on the 200 queries except fragments that are not words (expected improvement), cost per search 2 ms. Removes 1,164 files and one request per word.

**Step 6 (only if the test set shows a gap). Subsequence as a last-resort suggestion.**
- A 30-line function over the list: keys of 4+ aksara units (not code points), at most 2 skipped, top 3, anchored on the first letter, never silent, never retrieves results. Adv 1 puts the ceiling of gain at about +3 points overall; build it only if real failed queries show multi-letter omissions.

**What NOT to build.** A static subsequence index in any form; a folded variant index (B-full); a precomputed delete-neighbourhood file; the separate skeleton and fold JSON maps (derive them from the list); a delete index at load (linear scan is enough); edit distance 2 (73 candidates per query, 917 KB of tag files for the top 20, per Adv 1); substring shards at 3 letters; a rebuild of `tags/` from the texts right now; fixed weights for subsequence scoring that nobody can validate.

**Smallest first slice that delivers value:** step 0 plus step 1 (the harness and the top-50 cap on a sorted substring folder). It is one script and ten lines of search code, needs no new file shipped to visitors, reproduces a 4 to 20 times reduction in request count for common words, and it is a hard prerequisite for step 3.

## 5. What to measure before and after (summary)

- Per query: files fetched, gzipped KB, average, p50, p99, for 1-word and 3-word queries, and for the 8 worst particles; credits per 1,000 cold searches with Adv 2's formula (20 per GB, 2 per 10,000 requests).
- Quality: hadis found against uncapped (target at least 99%), top-10 on the real test set (no regression), recall at 1, 5, 20 per corruption type for the typo set, the number of correct rare words that receive a suggestion (must not rise above today's 39.5% of rare words that could, ideally fall), share of suggestions clicked can't be measured (no analytics by design), so use the test set.
- Mode identity: 2-letter mode against 3-letter mode, same hadis and ranking, except the cap.
- Cost: list size over the wire (232 to 351 KB gzipped), worker build time for derived maps, parse time, memory in the worker (measure on a real or throttled phone profile if possible; all timings here are Node).

## 6. Caveats of this judgement

- Every simulated typo rate (mine and the advisors') uses a synthetic error model; there are no real query logs. The ranking of methods is robust (subsequence equals edit distance 1 on drops, loses on everything else); absolute percentages are not.
- My drop test counts a typo only if the result is not a vocabulary word; 42% of single-letter drops of count-weighted words are words already (noisy vocabulary), a regime where the suggestion layer may not fire.
